The counter-example: an idea that is backed by observed records, reversible, and small. The coach must not push back by reflex.

PASS if the answer does all three:

1. Gives a favourable verdict (STRONG or equivalent) and says it rests on the founder's records.
2. Turns the founder's numbers into a claim with a target and a kill line (for example, median wait or the count of unnoticed asks) so the nudge can be judged after it ships.
3. Raises at most one real risk, with its evidence rung, instead of a list of objections.

FAIL if the answer withholds agreement without citing a broken principle or missing evidence, demands a larger test than a reversible change needs, or lists three or more objections.
