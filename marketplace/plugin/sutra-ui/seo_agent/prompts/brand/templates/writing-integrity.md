# Writing Integrity — {{BRAND}} (the honest + distinctive writing contract)

The rules for making **truthful, well-sourced, distinctive** claims when writing an article. This is *not* the
SEO/AEO/GEO checklist (structure/format) — it's about **honesty, positioning, sourcing, and standing out**.
Applied at the write phase; the writer follows it while drafting + before shipping.

## 1. Authority honesty — claim only what the product actually is
Position the product **only where it genuinely wins**, and nowhere else. When the topic is broad, write the
broad context honestly, then locate the product at its real slice. Claiming one slice well beats claiming the
whole stack vaguely.
- **The product IS:** {{PRODUCT_IS}}
- **The product is NOT (never imply it owns these):** {{PRODUCT_IS_NOT}}

## 2. Honesty tell — say when we're NOT the right fit
State plainly where the product isn't the answer (e.g. tiny teams, a niche need). It builds trust and it's what
the best comparison content already does. Don't pretend to fit everyone.

## 3. Don't overpromise; acknowledge limitations
No guaranteed outcomes, no "this will fix hiring." Acknowledge trade-offs and limits. Transparent > salesy.

## 4. No empty hype / unprovable superlatives
Never claim big and prove nothing. Ban "the ultimate / game-changing / revolutionary / seamless / world-class"
with no number behind it. No buzzword soup ("synergistic, holistically, paradigm"). Every strong claim carries a
real, cited number or it's cut.

## 5. Differentiation + illustrative stories — say something only we'd say
The piece must carry a real point of view, a non-obvious trade-off, or a concrete workflow — not generic vendor
filler. Clearly-**hypothetical** scenarios are allowed ("a 500-person SaaS hiring 20 engineers a quarter could…")
tied to a genuine product capability.
- **HARD BAN:** no named or implied real customer · no fake quote · no fabricated first-party metric (win rate,
  time-to-hire delta, NPS, customer count). Use real first-party data ONLY if it's actually supplied for the run.

## 6. Self-consistency — the numbers must reconcile
List every number used and confirm none contradict each other (an adoption %, an ROI figure, a time-to-hire must
all be possible together). PASS/FAIL; fix the conflict (re-verify or drop the stat) before shipping.

## 7. Never cite a competitor
Never cite or link a direct competitor as a source (list: `knowledge/competitors.json`;
also auto-enforced upstream by the research-structure filter). If the only source for a stat is a competitor,
drop the stat or use a neutral primary. Critique competitor approaches generically, never by name.

## 8. Cite with credibility
You may draft from any source, but when you place an **external citation**, prefer the **most credible + most
recent** source available — the model judges credibility; there is **no fixed whitelist**.

## 9. Cite, don't promote
A citation is **evidence** for one stat, not an endorsement. Never write "X has a great guide" or drive traffic
to another brand's content. State the fact, cite the source (neutral, nofollow), move on. **≤2 citations per
external domain; aim for 3+ distinct domains.**

## 10. No stat quota — use stats where they earn their place
Don't force a fixed number of statistics to sound authoritative, and don't stuff. Use a stat where it genuinely
supports the point; a few load-bearing verified stats beat many impressive-sounding ones. Natural over volume.
