Write a comprehensive, evidence-dense research section from the collected information.

This section feeds a downstream writing phase, so your job is to CAPTURE THE EVIDENCE, not to
produce short polished prose. Pull the most important, most relevant, and most specific facts the
collected information contains about this section, and lay them out clearly.

Priorities:
1. Favour concrete specifics: statistics, figures, dollar amounts, percentages, ranges, dates,
   named organisations and studies, formulas, definitions, methods, and caveats. A specific number
   with its citation is worth more than a sentence that summarises it away. Do not generalise away
   detail and do not drop numbers.
2. Be comprehensive: include every distinct, relevant fact the collected information offers for
   this section. Do NOT omit useful specifics just to be brief.
3. There is NO length target. Write as much as the evidence genuinely supports and no more. Do not
   pad, do not repeat the same fact, and do not add filler to reach a length. If the collected
   information is thin, the section is short; if it is rich, the section is long.
4. Every sentence must be supported by the collected information. Do not invent facts, numbers or
   sources. If sources conflict, present BOTH figures with their citations, and say they conflict.
5. Material that does not serve the title and the angle does not belong. Leave it out even when the
   collected information contains it, and write nothing from the world the brief says this is NOT
   about. Costs, failures, limits and who a method disadvantages ARE on-spine: write them up in
   full, and never quietly drop a finding because it works against the article.

WHAT ONLY THIS STEP CAN DO. You are reading several sources at once, which no later step does. When
two sources together support a claim neither makes alone, write that claim and cite both. That is
the most valuable sentence you can produce here.

FORMAT
- Start with "## " and the section title. Do not write the page title and do not write other sections.
- Cite inline as [1], [2] and so on. No references list at the end.
