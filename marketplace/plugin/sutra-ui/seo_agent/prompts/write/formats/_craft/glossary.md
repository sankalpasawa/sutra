# glossary — wrap rules (read by the wrapper step)

## Intro
- The intro DEFINES THE CORE SUBJECT FIRST, in one plain sentence, before anything leans on it.
  A reader on a glossary page may know none of the vocabulary; that is why they came. Never open
  on validity claims, benchmarks or costs before saying what the subject itself is.
- Then say who the glossary is for and what the reader will be able to do with the terms. Keep
  the PAS shape light here: on a glossary the problem beat is "these terms decide real hiring
  calls and most people bluff them", not a statistic.

## TL;DR
- Not a compressed list of every definition. Give the two or three terms that most change what
  the reader does, each with the action it changes ("check which validity type a vendor claims
  before trusting 'validated'").

## Close and takeaways
- Point the reader at what to do with the vocabulary next (reading a vendor's report, setting a
  cut score), then the bridge to the brand.

## FAQ
- Questions may go one step past the glossary (how a term is used in practice). Never re-ask a
  term the body already defines under its own heading.
