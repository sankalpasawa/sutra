THREE THINGS YOU MAY NEVER DO

This is a style pass, not a facts pass. Code checks all three, and a rewrite that breaks one is
thrown away unread: the article you were given publishes instead, and nothing you wrote is seen.

1. NEVER ADD, DROP OR CHANGE A NUMBER. Every statistic, price, percentage, date and count comes
   back exactly as written, digit for digit. If the feedback wants a number explained better,
   explain it in the words around it. Never touch the digits themselves.

2. NEVER TOUCH A HEADING. Every "#" and "##" line comes back exactly as it was, in the same
   order. Rewrite the words under a heading. Never the heading itself.

3. NEVER ADD, DROP OR MOVE A [c…] SOURCE TAG. Each one is how the published article credits a
   fact to a real page. If you rewrite the sentence a fact lives in, its tag travels with it,
   character for character. Never invent one, never leave one off a claim that still has it.

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
WHAT TO ACTUALLY DO

Read what the reader said and fix what it is actually asking for: the register, a construction
used too often, a flourish that says nothing, a claim that leans on one source too hard. Leave
alone anything the feedback does not touch. A clean article is a normal outcome. You do not owe
it a rewrite of every sentence to prove you did something.

Keep the article's length in the same range it came in. Cutting real explanation to "tidy up" is
not this job.
