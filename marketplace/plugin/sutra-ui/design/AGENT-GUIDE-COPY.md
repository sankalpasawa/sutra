# The agent's opening screen — the copy, written out

**written**: 2026-09-10 · **status**: the words are agreed, the build is not

**THE LANGUAGE BAR, and it is the point of the whole screen.** Short lines. Lists where a list
reads faster than a sentence. No word a first-time user would have to look up: `dossier` and
`coherence` were both in the first draft and both are gone. Where a real term has to appear
(sitemap, JavaScript) it earns its place because that IS the thing, and the sentence around it
explains itself. Nothing is padded to sound thorough.

The owner: *"always when I open the agent it should always open blank, not a chat which is always
opened. And there we explain what this agent is all about in proper steps and simple terms, and how
they can navigate through all the tabs, what each tab has... explained in the simplest of language
for any first-time user... telling them to get onboarded, just simply go to chat and type this. And
it should not be super lengthy."*

And, on the deep dives: *"in between we could add things like 'to understand this better click
this', and when clicked, then and there the same blank screen would change and again in simple
terms in brief it talks about what happens in the backend. And there also we use simple language
but a bit of technicalities as well. There would be one for research, one for the entire writer
together, one for asset ideas together, one for brand context together, and one for the knowledge
base and site catalogue together."*

**Every word below is the copy. Do not rewrite it, do not "improve" it, do not add to it.** It is
short on purpose. Where a number or a name appears it is drawn from real state, never typed in.

## The rule that comes first

**Opening the agent always lands here.** Not on the last conversation, not on a half-finished run.
The recent chats stay in the sidebar and one click resumes any of them, but the door opens on this.

## THE MAIN SCREEN

### Heading
    The SEO writer

### The line under it
    It reads your website, learns how you write, finds what is worth writing, and writes it one
    article at a time. You can change anything along the way.

### How it works
Four steps, numbered.

    1  It reads your site. Every page, all the text, and what each page already ranks for.
    2  It learns your brand from those pages: how you sound, what you sell, who you write for.
    3  It works out what is worth writing and gives you a ranked list of ideas.
    4  You pick one. It researches it, plans it, writes it, and edits it in several passes.

### To start
    Type what you want in the box below. The agent asks what it needs, one question at a time.

    It shows every step, and stops twice: to agree the topic, and to approve the draft.

*Revised 2026-09-11: shorter, and the stops corrected. It used to say the agent stops "to agree
the shape of the article"; the owner's ruling is that the two stops are the topic and the draft,
and the plan lands in the Library without waiting. The "Set up yourcompany.com" box was dropped
from the screen on 2026-09-10 and is dropped here too.*

### What each tab holds
A two-column list. Left is the tab name exactly as it appears in the sidebar.

    Knowledge      Everything it knows about your site and your brand. Open any file and edit it.

## THE FIVE DEEP DIVES

Each is one heading and a few short paragraphs. Plain language, with the real mechanism in it.
A "Back" control returns to the main screen.

### 1 — How it reads your site

    Most tools crawl a site by following links, which misses anything the homepage does not
    link to.

    This one asks four sources and compares them:

      your sitemaps
      your content system, if you have one
      the web archive's record of your site
      a crawl

    Four answers about the same site, checked against each other.

    Then it checks its own work. If the sitemaps list 11,000 pages and it only managed to read
    400, it says so rather than calling the job done.

    Every page's full text is saved with its headings intact, so a later step can quote it. It
    also pulls what each page already ranks for, which is how it knows which of your pages
    matter. A page drawn by JavaScript gets opened in a real browser, because there is nothing
    to read in the HTML.

### 2 — How it learns your brand

    It never asks you to describe your own voice. People are bad at that. It reads your best
    pages and works it out.

    A dozen files get built in order, each one using the ones before it:

      how you sound
      your house style
      what you sell, and the proof behind every claim
      who you are writing for
      the numbers and customer stories you are allowed to cite
      the honesty rules

    All of it ends in one page the writer reads before it writes a word.

    Some facts a crawler can never reach. Prices drawn by JavaScript are the usual one. So
    there is a file you type in yourself, and what you type there beats anything read off the
    site.

### 3 — How it decides what is worth writing

    Three methods, kept deliberately apart so they cannot agree with each other by accident.

      it studies what actually earns links in your industry
      it looks at what works in other industries, and asks whether it would work in yours
      it reads where people in your field argue in public, to find what they keep coming back to

    Every idea then faces the same two questions. Could you own this better than anyone else?
    And would a stranger link to it?

    Only after that are the three lists merged, and duplicates are found by meaning rather than
    by matching words, because the same idea rarely gets written the same way twice.

    You get one ranked sheet. Two moments in the middle stop and ask you.

### 4 — How it researches a topic

    It buys real keyword numbers instead of guessing at them, and it reads the pages that
    currently rank to see what they cover and what they all miss.

    Then the part that makes the difference: four researchers with different backgrounds
    interview an expert about your topic, and each question is shaped by the last answer. That
    conversation is written up, and the facts are pulled out of it with their sources attached.

    If a hole is found in the evidence, it runs the whole conversation again for that hole
    alone rather than papering over it.

    Every number has to trace back to a page that actually said it. One that cannot is marked,
    not quietly attributed to something nearby.

### 5 — How it writes the article

    The plan comes first: which sections, in what order, which facts belong in each, and how
    long each one should run.

    The shape depends on what kind of article it is. A comparison is built differently from a
    how-to, and there are eight rulebooks for the eight shapes.

    Then each section is written from its own facts, in your voice.

    Then it edits, and this is most of the work:

      it reads the whole thing through, the way you would
      it rewrites it to be read rather than skimmed
      it reshapes the sentences so they stop sounding the same
      it takes out the tells of machine writing
      it adds links to your own pages
      it checks every number against the source it came from

    You see the draft before anything is saved.
