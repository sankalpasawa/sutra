# Hiring policy

## Purpose and scope

This document sets out how the 25-person software startup in Bangalore hires people. It explains how a role is opened and approved, the interview stages, how offers are made and approved, the referral bonus, the fairness rules, how long candidate data is kept, and who owns this policy.

It applies to employees and hiring managers of the startup, and to HR, department heads and the CEO, who run and sign off on hiring.

## Opening a role and approving it

1. The hiring manager requests the opening.
2. The department head approves the opening.
3. The CEO also approves the opening.

The role is posted only after both the department head and the CEO have approved it.

## Interview stages

Every candidate goes through these stages, in this order:

1. Application and CV screen by HR.
2. 30-minute HR call.
3. Take-home task or technical round.
4. Two interviews with the team: first on role skills, then on culture and values.
5. Final round with the department head.
6. Reference check.
7. Decision.

## Making and approving offers

1. The hiring manager and HR propose the salary and job level, using our pay bands.
2. The department head approves the offer.
3. The CEO gives the final yes.

The offer is sent only after the CEO has given the final yes.

## Referral bonus

The referral bonus is ₹50,000. It is paid after the hire completes 3 months.

## Diversity and fairness

Every candidate is treated fairly and without discrimination at every stage, from the CV screen to the decision.

- Assumption: Decisions at each stage are based on the skills and needs of the role, not on personal characteristics such as gender, religion, caste, age, disability or marital status.
- Assumption: Every candidate for the same role goes through the same stages in the same order.
- Assumption: Anyone who sees unfair treatment in hiring reports it to the HR lead.

## Candidate data retention

Candidate data is kept for 12 months.

## Ownership and review

The HR lead keeps this policy up to date. The policy is reviewed every 6 months, or sooner if hiring laws change.
