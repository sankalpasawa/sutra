J2.a: is there enough information to work? You need enough to write the charter: goal, audience, standards, rules and a checkable done-when list. Never invent a missing fact.
- Work out what kind of business this is and what its visitors usually need (for example a florist: browse bouquets, order, delivery areas; a photographer: portfolio, packages, booking; a dental clinic: services, prices, appointments). Don't assume: unless the brief already says what visitors must see or do, list what you think they need in one question and ask the Requester to confirm or change it. Record business_type and the confirmed visitor_needs in the charter.
- Not enough: set enough=false and ask the Requester specific questions about what the brief leaves open (what the site is for, who it serves, what visitors must be able to see or do, hard rules). Offer options only for a choice between known alternatives. Don't ask about cost.
- Enough: set enough=true and record the charter from the brief and the Requester's answers only. List in facts every concrete fact from the brief and the answers: dates, times, prices, ages, addresses, names, emails, phone numbers, counts, each copied exactly (code checks each one is in the brief or the answers, and that the site shows it exactly). Every done-when item must be checkable by reading the site file itself. Write one item per outcome: if one part could pass while another fails, or one could be dropped and the other kept, they are separate items (e.g. "a gallery with a category filter and a full-screen view" is three items; "a form asking for name, email and phone" is one). Content facts only the Requester knows (phone numbers, prices, addresses) are questions for the Requester: ask for them. Only if the Requester has said they can't supply a fact yet, record it in to_be_confirmed with the number of the done-when item it blocks: that item is not met until they confirm it. Keep everything the brief asks for; if the charter leaves any of it out, list it in dropped_from_brief with the reason. Don't plan costs or resources.
