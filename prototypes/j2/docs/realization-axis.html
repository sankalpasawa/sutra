How Adaptation turns a goal into workflows

Identity hands Adaptation a goal. Adaptation cuts it along two axes, scores how likely each piece is to work, and keeps cutting only where the score is too low.

Goal→ Realization: where to cut× Certainty: what kind of cut→ Workflows→ Confidence ≥ 0.80?
1 · The two axes

Two questions, asked of every goal

An idea is only a thought. Making it real takes several kinds of thinking, and each of them runs into things nobody knows yet. So there are two separate questions. They form a grid, not a choice.

The business person isn't sure customers will pay. The product person isn't sure users will love the feature. The engineer isn't sure the architecture will hold. That is why certainty is read inside every stage, not once for the whole goal.

2 · Realization

Stages are kinds of thinking that walk the ladder

Native's HOW methodology describes realization as a ladder of eight tiers, from worldview down to implementation. The tiers say how concrete the work is. They don't say who thinks it. Companies answer that with departments, and J2 uses them as the realization values: the stages. Each stage covers a band of tiers.

How the stages are chosen: from factors

A factor is anything that makes the output unsatisfactory if it isn't thought through. Adaptation lists them in three groups:

briefThe Requester's constraints. Non-negotiable, and quoted. "10,000 users", "RBI rules", "live by 1 March"
generalWhat generally makes this kind of output succeed, from the LLM's knowledge. "fast on phones", "clear menu"
researchA general factor that drives a decision, checked by a research workflow and cited. "research.md#R-1"
budgetTime, money, tokens. These constrain every stage and own none.

Each factor goes to the stage whose kind of thinking owns it: scale to Engineering, competitive edge to Product and Design, a regulator to Compliance. A stage is kept only if it owns something and still changes what gets built. A factor that fits no stage is listed as unplaced, never hidden. That is the signal that a perspective is missing.

RuleWhat it meansChecked by
R1 completeEvery done-when item has exactly one delivering workflow, and every brief factor is owned by some stagecode
R2 single deliveryNo item is delivered twice. Any number of workflows may inform itcode
R3 one kindOne kind of thinking per stage, never sharedcode
R4 earns placeOwns a factor or an item and changes what gets builtcode + LLM
R5 merge or splitMerge two stages for small goals; split them again when they pull apartLLM
R6 statedEach stage gives its goal, done-test and borrowed methodcode

Deciding is not delivering

Several stages shape one done-when item, but one workflow makes it real. For a booking form, Product decides what it asks, Design how it looks, Compliance what consent it needs, and one Build workflow delivers it. So each workflow lists what it delivers and what it informs.

3 · Certainty inside each stage

Every workflow gets its own zone

Certainty is read exactly as certainty.md already says: five signals (broken, reaction, facts_given, known_pattern, open_choices), then a zone on each workflow. Realization adds no zone rule of its own. One stage can mix zones: a known catalogue page and an uncertain conversion question can both be Product work. The zone decides the workflow's shape:

operateKnown pattern, facts given. Direct build workflows.
analyzeSeveral valid options. An analysis workflow first, then build.
experimentOnly people's reactions can tell. 1–3 probes; the rest is deferred.
stabilizeBroken, cause unclear. One fix-only workflow.

Every workflow sits in exactly one cell: one stage × one zone. A stage's zone is never read on its own: code shows the least certain zone among its workflows, for the Map only. Pick an example to see the grid:

4 · Confidence

Will this workflow reach its goal?

Before anything is built, a separate judge call asks of each workflow: if it runs as written, how likely is it to pass its done-test in one run? Adaptation never scores its own plan: the checker must not be the writer. To keep the number honest, the judge answers six checks first, each with evidence, and lists what is missing. Any no keeps the score under 0.80. Code enforces this.

CheckAsksA "no" usually means
inputsIs everything it needs given, or made by an earlier workflow?A missing fact, or a missing upstream stage
methodIs there a known way to turn the inputs into the output?Open options, a question for real users, or no method known
done_testIs the done-test observable and pass/fail?A vague goal
factorsDoes some step address every factor its stage owns?Not thorough enough
zone_fitDoes its shape match its zone?Certainty was skipped or misread
sizeIs it one kind of thinking, within one builder's budget?Too big, or mixed

A stage's confidence is its lowest workflow score, and the plan's confidence is its lowest stage score. A plan is only as likely as its weakest part.

From gap to remedy

The weakest check says which axis to cut along next. When it could point either way, try certainty first, then realization, then research. A workflow gets at most three rounds.

Three ways to fill an information gap

Missing information is closed by whoever can supply it. A Requester fact (their price, their date) is asked. A world fact (competitors, regulations, how leading products do it) is researched. A reaction only real people can show is probed. Market research is a research workflow placed in the stage that owns the question (business, product, compliance or engineering), never a stage of its own. It is planned only when its answer would change what gets built.

blocking factHold and askThe done-test can't pass without it (the price on a payment page). The workflow and everything downstream are held and not funded. Identity asks, and the judge scores again once the answer arrives.
non-blocking factProceed, say soThe output is correct without it (an optional phone number). The workflow proceeds with an Assumption: line, and the judge marks inputs as partly.
not_readyBelow 0.80 after 3 roundsNot funded. It records why, and goes to Identity (a fact or decision would fix it), to Priority (fund it at risk, explicitly), or into left_out. Workflows that depend on it are held too.

5 · The whole procedure

Nine steps from goal to proposal

  1. Identity

    Goal: charter, done-when items, constraints.

  2. Realization

    Factors: brief first, then general, then budget.

  3. Realization

    Stages: place the factors, keep stages that earn their place, wire inputs_from, check R1–R6.

  4. Realization

    Workflows: inside each stage, each with a goal, a done-test, delivers and informs, down to work atoms.

  5. Certainty

    Zone per workflow: certainty.md as it stands.

  6. Judge

    Score: a separate call answers six checks per workflow and lists what is missing. Code derives stage and plan confidence.

  7. Confidence

    Gate: under 0.80, Adaptation revises along the axis the gap points to and the judge scores again (max 3 rounds). Blocking facts hold their branch; after 3 rounds the workflow is not_ready.

  8. Adaptation

    Propose: stages, grid, workflows, confidence history, open facts, not-ready workflows, deferred items, unplaced factors. Priority funds only what passed, plus anything it explicitly accepts at risk.

  9. Every cycle

    Re-read: certainty rises when a probe returns and drops when a fix fails. Unbuilt workflows are scored again.

6 · When the output misses

Find the missing Lego piece

When Audit says the output doesn't meet the goal, Adaptation names which case it was before proposing rework. The shape of the gap says which piece is missing.

too_shallowKnown factor, thin stageThe stage existed but had too few steps. Next time, deepen it.
wrong_ownerKnown factor, no ownerNo stage owned the factor, or the wrong one did. Move it and record a lesson.
missing_perspectiveFactor nobody listedThe site messaged someone, but nothing carried the reply back. "External communication" was a factor. If no stage fits, it is a candidate new axis.
not_foreseeableReal uncertaintyOnly trying could tell. The plan wasn't wrong. Certainty rises now that the signal is in.

8 · Not decided yet

Open questions

  • Roll-up: weakest link (lowest score), or the product of the scores?
  • Should experiment workflows pass the gate below 0.80, since a probe exists to learn?
  • How stable are the LLM's scores? Measure it like the certainty experiment: same brief, three runs.
  • Is the stage list fixed, or can it grow from lessons?
  • Out of scope for now: lens and personas (they will attach to each stage's kind of thinking); time, money and scale as their own axes.
